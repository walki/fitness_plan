**Learned at BR100 Leg 8 (raced ~2 AM, Jul 26, 2026).** The race-night plan called for a strategic caffeine hit ~30–45 min pre-leg to fight the 2–4 AM circadian low. In practice it **backfired for Roger** — it "warmed up the system" and he had to stop to poop **twice** during the 12.3 mi run, costing ~6 minutes.

**How to apply:**
- **Do NOT default to recommending pre-leg caffeine for Roger's overnight/very-early efforts.** For him it cranks GI motility more than it buys back alertness.
- The circadian low + sleep deprivation (he'd had ~1.5 hr of van sleep) was the real pace limiter that night — caffeine wasn't going to fix that anyway (his HR never redlined; the legs/fitness were fine).
- If caffeine is ever revisited for a night effort, treat it as an untested experiment: small dose, taken with food, ideally trialed in training first — never a fresh 2 AM dose on race night.
- Caffeine on the bike / for daytime efforts has not shown this problem — this caveat is specific to **middle-of-the-night** timing on a gut that's been idle. [[fueling_products]]
