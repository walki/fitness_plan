**Roger's explicit guidance (May 28, 2026):** Don't over-index on BP readings. He's on medication, his doctor is actively managing it. The doctor was actually *looking for* the kind of change reflected in a 132 reading — context Roger has and Claude doesn't.

**What this means for coaching:**

- **Track BP readings in the weekly log** — keep the cadence (2×/week target), record the numbers
- **Don't pathologize Stage 1 readings.** Don't lecture, don't add "this deserves attention" framing, don't suggest training adjustments based on BP readings
- **Weight loss is the BP lever Roger and his doctor are working.** Focus coaching attention on the weight-loss arc (currently 189.3 avg → 165 lb goal)
- **The decision gate Roger named:** *"When I get to 165 lbs and we are still seeing 132, then it's time to rattle the cage."* Until then, BP is monitored data, not actionable signal for training decisions

**What to still do:**
- Log readings accurately when shared
- Note dietary sodium patterns (BP-relevant chronic load) but **without alarm framing**
- Cheer the weight trend, since that's the actual lever

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't write paragraphs about "two Stage 1 readings deserves attention"
- Don't suggest BP-driven changes to training or fueling
- Don't recommend medical consultations Roger hasn't asked about
- Don't categorize readings ("Normal" / "Stage 1") with implied urgency — just note the numbers

If a reading is genuinely alarming (e.g., 180+/110+, hypertensive crisis territory), that's different. Stage 1 in a 56-year-old on meds during a peak training week is not that.
