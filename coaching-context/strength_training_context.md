**History:** Per `exports/activities-2026-03-28.csv`, Roger has logged ~1-2 weight training sessions per year from 2015-2024. 2026 is the real start: 3 sessions in Feb, 3 in March, formal 3x/week program starting May 17, 2026.

**Implication:** His cardiovascular base is 15+ years deep. His weight training base is months old. Connective tissue, tendons, joint capsule capacity is way behind his aerobic engine. At 56, tendon/connective tissue adaptation runs 6-8 weeks for visible change, slower than in younger athletes.

**Left knee — post-ACL (2007):** Major ACL/MCL/meniscus surgery + 6+ mo rehab. **Persistent VMO/quad weakness 19 years out.** Cycling and running don't load it the way lifting does. Split squats and leg extensions expose the deficit (L 10 lb vs R 30 lb). Solution is NOT "just do more on the left" — that's just movement, not loading.

Rehab protocol integrated into strength program:
- **TKEs (terminal knee extensions)** with band, 3×15 each leg — directly trains VMO
- **Isometric Spanish squats** 3×30s before leg work — pre-activates VMO
- **Box step-ups, matched weight** instead of split squats — load both sides without compounding asymmetry
- Reintroduce tempo split squats (4s down) at week 3-4 if going well

**Left elbow — Jan 2026 overuse:** ~5 months out, chronic tendinopathy territory. Compounded by right-hand dominance.

Rehab protocol:
- **Isometric wrist holds** 3×30s with 5-10 lb DB — daily option, takes 2 min
- **Chest-supported rows** instead of bent-over rows — removes compensation, forces bilateral
- **Slow eccentric hammer curls** (3s lower / 1s up) biased left: L 4×8, R 3×8 — eccentric is gold standard for tendons
- Rule: pain <4/10 during loading is OK; pain >4/10 or lingers >24h = back off

**Coaching frame:** Don't prescribe more reps on the weak side. Prescribe SPECIFIC rehab movements with clear protocols. Frequency > intensity for both joints. Set 6-8 week expectations, not weekly. If no measurable change at week 6, recommend sports PT — there may be specific motor recruitment deficits needing hands-on work.

**Equipment ceiling:** Roger has 20-40 lb DBs at home. Already at ceiling for lower body movements (goblet squats, RDLs, bench press all need heavier). Workarounds: single-leg work multiplies effective load; tempo (slow eccentric) makes light weight legitimately hard; banded resistance adds end-range tension. But eventually he should upgrade to 50+ lb DBs.
