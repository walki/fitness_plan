**Established Jun 17, 2026** after Roger reported dropping shoulder press + curl from 25 lb → 20 lb on set 2 ("at the heavier weight I was flopping around a little, trying to be good with form over just slinging weight around") and intentionally swapping wrist hold to 5 lb × 42s for time-under-tension variation.

**The principle:** Roger is past "follow the prescription" and into "understand the prescription." He's making good autoregulation decisions:
- Drops weight when form breaks (set 2 of press/curl)
- Varies duration vs load for tendon work (IWH)
- Knows when 25 lb is "form ceiling" vs "ego ceiling"

**Coaching implication:** Don't micromanage weights in weekly prescriptions. Set:
- The **movement** (DB shoulder press, hammer curl, etc.)
- The **rep range and set count** (3×10, etc.)
- The **intent** (slow eccentric, fast tempo, isometric hold, etc.)

Let Roger set the load by feel and form. This is faster, more honest, more sustainable.

**Form-over-weight rule (now permanent):** Drop the weight when form breaks. Don't grind out ugly reps. "Flopping around a little" at a target weight = working *just above* form ceiling, which is where strength growth lives — but the rep itself should still execute cleanly. Set 2 drop is the right call.

**Progressions go by:**
- "Can do X × 2 sets clean" → graduate to X × 3 sets
- Movement / TUT variations are fine and welcome — combined stimulus trends up over time
- Don't push raw load just to push it

**Same principle extends beyond strength:** Roger reads HR ceilings on runs and dials back. He scaled the over-under wattage during sharpen week instead of grinding. He walked steep pitches at FBG instead of pushing. The pattern is **"listen to the body, decide intelligently."** Coach's job is the framework, not the moment-to-moment knob-turning.

**What this rules OUT:**
- "Push-ups 2×10 @ exactly 25 lb"
- "Don't drop the weight"
- "You should be able to do X by now"

**What this still allows:**
- Setting movement progression milestones ("when 25×3 clean, move to 30")
- Noting target capacity ("aim for plank 60s by end of month")
- Flagging when a load looks too low for the body's actual capacity ("the 15 lb hammer curl might be ready to go to 20")
